## Derivation Rules

The framework was re-derived directly from the 18 eligible distillations. The
corresponding PDFs are adjudication sources only: consult them to resolve
ambiguity or verify a physical page reference, not to expand the corpus.

During construction, load-bearing page references were visually spot-checked
against the source PDFs for Edmans (PDF p. 3), Tihanyi (PDF p. 2), Corley and
Gioia (PDF p. 6), Alvesson and Sandberg (2011, PDF p. 12), Leahey, Lee, and Funk
(PDF p. 18), Howard-Grenville, Vasudeva, and Yiu (PDF p. 6), Colquitt and George
(PDF p. 2), and Cochrane (PDF p. 3). The remaining citations follow the
distillations' verified physical-page evidence maps.

The following pre-existing materials did **not** provide substantive evidence:

- cross-paper synthesis;
- cluster syntheses or audits;
- field guide;
- slide deck;
- prospectus template; and
- memo index.

Do not use those materials to update this skill.

Every substantive framework claim must cite an eligible author-year source and
physical PDF page. Candidate-specific claims must instead cite the user's idea,
draft, supplied closest paper, or verified `search-literature` map entry. Never
use a framework citation as evidence that a particular candidate is factually
novel.

## Runtime Boundary

- Never use outside literature to update the fixed diagnostic framework.
- Candidate-specific closest papers may be supplied by the user or discovered
  through the auditable `search-literature` workflow. They establish only the
  comparison for that candidate and do not alter the diagnostic criteria.
- Never browse or search databases ad hoc while applying the assessor.
- Do not search for a broad-interest-only assessment or when Christian requests
  a source-bounded assessment.
- When no adequate comparator evidence is available, assess only the
  articulation of the leap and state exactly what factual novelty remains
  unverified.
- Assume correctness; do not audit identification, measurement, methods, or
  execution.
- Do not copy source PDFs into this repository or an assessment output.

## Epistemic Status

The corpus combines conceptual essays, editorials, retrospective advice, surveys,
and observational studies. Much of it explains how experienced scholars
recognize interesting, important, original, or influential work; it does not
prospectively validate a selection rule. Accordingly, the resulting framework is
an expert-derived diagnostic, not a calibrated forecast of publication,
citations, disruption, policy uptake, or welfare impact.
